# 摘要

## 【文档说明】

本摘要文档是硕士学位论文《基于多模态大语言模型的胶质母细胞瘤（GBM）新药候选分子生成与优化研究》的中英文摘要参考文本。统计数据均来源于 `gbm_project/final_results/` 中的原始CSV文件，**所有DrugClip与Vina统计数据仅针对DrugClip>0分子（预测有活性）**。

## 中文摘要

胶质母细胞瘤（Glioblastoma, GBM）是成人中最常见、最具侵袭性的原发性恶性脑肿瘤，其恶性程度高、异质性强、增殖与侵袭能力极强，预后极差。当前标准治疗策略（手术切除辅以放疗和替莫唑胺化疗）的中位生存期仅为12—15个月，五年生存率不足5%。血脑屏障（Blood-Brain Barrier, BBB）的存在构成了药物递送的巨大生理障碍，超过98%的小分子药物无法有效穿透BBB到达肿瘤部位。如何在保证BBB穿透性的前提下，同时优化分子的靶点结合活性、药代动力学性质和安全性，已成为GBM新药研发面临的核心挑战。

近年来，多模态大语言模型（Multi-modal Large Language Model, MLLM）的发展为解决上述挑战提供了新的技术路径。现有分子生成模型虽然在特定指标上表现优异，但普遍缺乏对GBM病理背景、临床治疗经验和多目标药代动力学约束的深度整合能力。针对这一问题，本文提出了一种基于多模态大语言模型Llamole的GBM候选药物端到端生成与优化框架，主要工作与贡献如下：

第一，针对GBM新药发现中多目标约束优化困难的问题，本文构建了一个集成GBM领域知识检索、多模态语义对齐、思维链（Chain-of-Thought, CoT）推理与思维树（Tree-of-Thoughts, ToT）搜索、基于物理引擎的虚拟筛选的端到端药物发现流程。该流程以Llamole作为控制核心，整合图扩散变换器（Graph Diffusion Transformer）和图神经网络（Graph Neural Network, GNN）实现分子图的生成与编码，并使用LoRA（Low-Rank Adaptation）参数高效微调技术实现领域适配。

第二，针对生成分子的可靠性验证问题，本文设计了一个融合分子对接（Docking）、ADMET（吸收、分布、代谢、排泄、毒性）预测和多目标合意度优化（Multi-Parameter Optimization, MPO） Reward的综合评估体系。该体系通过AutoDock Vina进行结合亲和力评估、使用Clark公式进行BBB穿透性预测，并结合肝毒性（DILI）和心脏毒性（hERG）风险的结构警报检测，实现了分子生成与物理验证的闭环反馈。

第三，针对生成分子的质量与结构多样性问题，本文提出了一种基于ToT的广度优先搜索（BFS）三阶段生成策略。该策略在骨架提议（scaffold proposal）、片段组装（fragment assembly）和SMILES生成三个阶段分别引入物理引擎反馈，引导模型逐步优化分子结构，显著提升了高潜力候选分子的生成比例。

第四，通过针对EGFR、VEGF/VEGFR和IDH1/IDH2三个代表性GBM靶点的系统实验验证，本文方法在综合评分上显著优于基线模型。在DrugClip>0分子（预测有活性）中：
- **DrugClip均值**：EGFR 0.57、IDH1 0.93、VEGF 0.62，综合均值 **0.74**
- **Vina对接均值**：EGFR -5.34 kcal/mol、IDH1 **-7.35 kcal/mol**、VEGF -3.89 kcal/mol
- **强结合比例（Vina<-7.0）**：EGFR 23.5%、IDH1 **64.1%**、VEGF 10.4%
- **BBB穿透通过率**：EGFR 47.5%、IDH1_IDH2 45.0%、VEGF_VEGFR **55.6%**，综合 **49.3%**
- **理化性质**：EGFR MW均值296.63、TPSA均值63.96；IDH1 MW均值391.84、TPSA均值80.09；VEGF MW均值352.21、TPSA均值66.78

消融实验表明，去除物理评估（DrugClip均值下降**24.0%**）、去除反馈（下降**20.1%**）、去除CoT推理（下降**15.8%**）三个模块对生成质量的损害最为显著，验证了各模块的必要性。

综上所述，本文提出的基于多模态大语言模型的GBM候选分子生成框架，在分子有效性、BBB穿透性、靶点结合活性等多个维度上展现出良好的性能，为GBM创新药物的早期发现提供了一条高效、可靠的数据驱动新路径。

**关键词**：胶质母细胞瘤；多模态大语言模型；分子生成；血脑屏障；树状思维搜索；AutoDock Vina；多目标优化

## English Abstract

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most common and aggressive primary malignant brain tumor in adults, characterized by high malignancy, strong heterogeneity, and extremely poor prognosis, with a median survival of only 12—15 months under the current standard treatment (surgery combined with radiotherapy and temozolomide chemotherapy). The Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) presents a formidable physiological obstacle to drug delivery, preventing over 98% of small-molecule drugs from effectively reaching tumor sites. How to optimize BBB penetration while simultaneously improving target binding affinity, pharmacokinetic properties, and safety has become a core challenge in GBM drug discovery.

Recent advances in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have provided a novel technical pathway to address the above challenges. Existing molecular generation models, although exhibiting strong performance on specific individual metrics, generally lack the capability for deep integration of GBM pathological backgrounds, clinical treatment experiences, and multi-objective pharmacokinetic constraints. To tackle this issue, this thesis proposes an end-to-end GBM drug candidate generation and optimization framework based on the multi-modal LLM Llamole. The main contributions are as follows:

First, to address the difficulty of multi-objective constraint optimization in GBM drug discovery, this thesis constructs an end-to-end drug discovery pipeline that integrates GBM domain knowledge retrieval, multi-modal semantic alignment, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) search, and physics-based virtual screening. The pipeline uses Llamole as the control core, integrating a Graph Diffusion Transformer and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for molecular graph generation and encoding, and employs Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) parameter-efficient fine-tuning for domain adaptation.

Second, to address the reliability verification of generated molecules, this thesis designs a comprehensive evaluation system that integrates molecular docking, ADMET (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, Toxicity) prediction, and Multi-Parameter Optimization (MPO) Reward. This system evaluates binding affinity using AutoDock Vina, predicts BBB permeability using the Clark formula, and detects hepatotoxicity (DILI) and cardiotoxicity (hERG) risks via structural alert detection, achieving a closed-loop feedback between molecular generation and physical verification.

Third, to address the quality and structural diversity of generated molecules, this thesis proposes a three-stage Breadth-First Search (BFS) strategy based on Tree-of-Thoughts. In the scaffold proposal, fragment assembly, and SMILES generation stages, physical engine feedback is introduced to guide the model in progressively optimizing molecular structures, significantly improving the generation rate of high-potential candidate molecules.

Fourth, through systematic experimental validation targeting three representative GBM targets — EGFR, VEGF/VEGFR, and IDH1/IDH2 — the proposed method demonstrates substantial superiority over baseline models in comprehensive scoring. Among molecules with DrugClip > 0 (predicted active):
- **DrugClip means**: EGFR 0.57, IDH1 0.93, VEGF 0.62, overall mean **0.74**
- **Vina docking means**: EGFR -5.34 kcal/mol, IDH1 **-7.35 kcal/mol**, VEGF -3.89 kcal/mol
- **Strong binding rate (Vina<-7.0)**: EGFR 23.5%, IDH1 **64.1%**, VEGF 10.4%
- **BBB penetration pass rate**: EGFR 47.5%, IDH1_IDH2 45.0%, VEGF_VEGFR **55.6%**, overall **49.3%**
- **Physicochemical properties**: EGFR MW mean 296.63, TPSA mean 63.96; IDH1 MW mean 391.84, TPSA mean 80.09; VEGF MW mean 352.21, TPSA mean 66.78

The ablation study indicates that removing physical evaluation (DrugClip mean drop **24.0%**), removing feedback (drop **20.1%**), and removing CoT reasoning (drop **15.8%**) are the most significant contributors to performance degradation, validating the necessity of each module.

In summary, the proposed multi-modal LLM-based GBM candidate molecule generation framework demonstrates strong performance across multiple dimensions including molecular validity, BBB permeability, and target binding affinity, providing an efficient and reliable data-driven pathway for early-stage GBM innovative drug discovery.

**Keywords**: Glioblastoma; Multi-modal Large Language Model; Molecular Generation; Blood-Brain Barrier; Tree-of-Thoughts; AutoDock Vina; Multi-Parameter Optimization
